01 — The problem

The facts are public. They are also scattered.

Schools name the products that they use, but each school does it in its own way. The facts sit in web pages, PDF files, Word documents and newsletters, on about 20,000 different websites.

Without Almanac

  • Each school publishes in its own format and in its own place.
  • Someone must open each website and each document, one at a time.
  • A check of about 20,000 schools by hand takes months.
  • The list is out of date before it is complete.

With Almanac

  • One dataset for every state school in England.
  • A consistent record for each product, with a link to its source.
  • Search, filter and compare by product, category, local authority or trust.
  • Termly updates show which schools adopted or dropped a product.

02 — How it works

One dataset that you can query.

Almanac does the slow work once, for every open state-funded mainstream school in England. You get consistent records that you can search, compare and track over time.

  1. 1

    We find the sources

    We find the public pages and documents in which each school names the products that it uses.

  2. 2

    We extract the facts

    Our software reads each source, in any format. It finds each product that a school names, puts it in its category and records how strongly the source shows use.

  3. 3

    You search and analyse

    Query every school in one place. Filter by product, category, local authority or trust, and export the results.

05 — How we work

Public sources. No personal data.

We record facts about schools as organisations. Every record shows where it came from.

  • Organisation-level facts only

    We record which products a school names. We do not record facts about people.

  • No personal data

    We do not record the names of staff or governors.

  • Every record links to its source

    Each mention links to the public page or document where the school names the product.

  • We do not republish documents

    We write a short note in our own words and link to the original.
